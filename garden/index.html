The Sixth Piggie — the quiet corner for the gardener's eye only

Five clues, one per chapter, tied by a blue thread — and the hidden chapter they open. Listed plainly here because this shelf is Sven's; a public shelf would veil them and let the hunt do its own work.

Clue 1 — the feast's sixth placeQuiet Five plates, and one more tucked behind the bread basket. A pear moon disappears; the napkin hem reads “Leave a little room.” Clue 2 — prints by the wellQuiet Six small prints cross the damp patch — five to market, one turning aside toward the thyme. Clue 3 — the hollow in the cushionQuiet Behind the mending basket, the window-seat cushion keeps one shallow hollow, as if someone small sat there a little while. Clue 4 — the tea party's sixth chairQuiet A tablecloth hem hangs a little longer on one side than it should. Behind it, a small chair — and a cup already poured, still warm. Clue 5 — the stone apartQuiet One stone apart beneath the willow, dry, with a blue ribbon and two close-set hoofmarks. Touch it, and the door opens. The hidden chapter — Mallow's Little WhileQuiet Mallow, soft-grey with one ear folded forward: “You can sit down while I think.” Six fireflies, one moth, tea for whoever is still thinking. Bonus — the sharing clothQuiet The embroidered piggie at the cloth's edge, her basket holding a feather and a fallen blossom. “Room for one more, always.”

El Phantea's Tea Letters postcards from a pocket elephant — six of six, one that arrives afterwards, one from the postmistress herself, and now one in footprints

Each letter is a tap-to-unfold postcard in the ic.lov manner, and each unlocks a tiny interaction in the book. The bundle is complete — and one more waits under the willow for readers who have found Mallow, and now the postmistress herself has written back, and Pipkin has written in footprints.

1 · Margo — dusk-roseLetter Three baskets under a striped awning, and a paper twist of the good rose. Mind the tablecloth. 2 · Wee — the hoofprint letterLetter Six neat trot marks and one smudged extra. Hop the brook stones; mind the sock and mind the door. 3 · Rocco — the feast noteLetter The quiet end of the bone, which makes a decent spoon — and keeps tea warm two minutes longer. 4 · Hazel — the cottage letterLetter A half-open door, lamplight on moss-green steps, and three thyme seeds — one for the sill, one for the soup, one to keep. 5 · Clove — the lavender bellsLetter Three silver bells, each tap ringing softer than the last, until the steam stands up straight. 6 · The brook stones — the world's own letterLetter Written by water on crinkled grey paper: the middle stone is kept warm for small feet. 7 · Mallow writes back — a reward letterQuiet The kettle is on the flat pebble and the moth has settled on the lid again. It arrives only after Mallow is found — the page veils itself until then. 8 · El Phantea writes back — the postmistress's own letterLetter The dusk-rose blend, the smallest cup first, and her own question: one lump, or two? Open to every reader — she delivers to all. 9 · Pipkin writes — in footprintsLetter Eight small footprints hopping to one pressed sprout. Tap it and he hops — "That tickles!", "Again! Again!" — and the bud quivers, still closed. Open to every reader.
