## Strand 2 — The Sixth Piggie *(ChatGPT lane)*

The lane's framing line, worth keeping verbatim: **"The sixth piggie should
feel like someone the pictures have always known. Finding her adds a room to
the book; it never repairs an incomplete ending."**

**The five clues**

1. **The market.** Beside the well, beneath the stallholder's hanging apron,
   six small hoofprints cross a damp patch. Five point toward the market; the
   last turns toward a clump of thyme. A tiny blue thread is caught on the
   well's lowest stone. Pipkin glances that way while the narration continues
   shopping. No hotspot outline, no reward sound — incidental illustration.
2. **Hazel's cottage.** On the window seat, behind the mending basket, a
   cushion with one shallow hollow. A blue thread trails from an unfinished
   embroidered pear. Touch the cushion and its corner settles; the curtain
   lifts enough to show a little ear-shaped shadow outside.
3. **Rocco's feast.** At the far end of the table, partly hidden by the bread
   basket, a sixth place: a small plate, a folded blue napkin, and a pear cut
   into thin moons. Nobody names or counts the places. During the feast one
   pear moon quietly disappears. Touching the napkin reveals a sentence
   stitched along its hem: **"Leave a little room."**
4. **The sharing chapter.** Where the changed rhyme appears on a hanging
   cloth, its bottom seam includes a tiny embroidered piggie carrying an empty
   basket, standing just beyond the last printed line. Touching her opens the
   basket's cloth cover: inside, a feather and one fallen blossom.
5. **The brook.** One stepping stone sits slightly apart from the route home,
   sheltered beneath a willow. On its dry surface: a blue ribbon and two
   close-set hoofmarks. Touching the stone turns its reflection into a warmly
   lit doorway for a moment, and a small handwritten line appears: *"There was
   room beside the rhyme."* Touching that line opens the hidden chapter;
   walking on finishes the familiar journey.

The lane's gentlest mechanic: **any earlier clue, touched a second time, can
also reveal that same line.** No required sequence, no completed set. Once
revealed, the line stays available on that page — including through a
discreet, descriptively labelled keyboard-focusable button.

**The hidden chapter — "Mallow's Little While"**

It opens beneath the willow at dusk. Roots make a low, round room; through
its leafy ceiling the reader can still see the brook's stepping stones. A
kettle stands on a flat pebble; six mismatched cups wait on a cloth.

**Mallow** is a small, soft-grey piggie with one ear that folds forward when
she listens. She likes the edges of gatherings, where she can hear both
conversation and birds. She carries spare string, notices uncomfortable
cushions, and cuts pears thin enough for everyone to have another piece. She
sometimes wants company before she knows what to say. Her first words are:
**"You can sit down while I think."**

Pipkin sits. The reader can place a cup beside either piggie. Mallow tries to
explain where she has been, then loses her sentence when a moth lands on the
kettle. They watch it together. After a little while, she says: *"I liked
walking with you."* If El Phantea is available she pours; otherwise Mallow
tips the kettle herself — nobody needs unlocking to receive tea.

The ending returns to the familiar homeward picture, now with Mallow beside
Pipkin. The narration says: *"And home they went, with room between them for
the evening."*

The printable keepsake gains a small extra illustration on its reverse:
Mallow's willow room, with a seventh cup left for the reader. Beneath it:
*"For whenever you have a little while."*

> **Sae's pick:** the feast clue — the sixth place with its pear moons and
> the napkin hem stitched *"Leave a little room."* It is the whole book's
> moral (sharing is making room) hidden in plain sight at the table, it works
> visually, tactilely and thematically at once, and it gives the hidden
> chapter its thesis before the reader ever finds the door. Mallow's name and
> her first line are keepers too.

## Strand 4 — The Sequel: *This Little Piggie Planted a Garden* *(Claude lane)*

**Frame (the lane's):** the book opens on the autumn cottage painting — pink
cottage, Wee in the red polka-dot dress at the garden gate, the sea behind,
an empty brown garden bed. It closes on the same painting a year later, with
the bed full and gone to seed.

**Ch. 1 — "This little piggie went to market… for seeds."**
Early autumn, apples dropping, first cool wind off the sea. Margo's seed
stall at the harbour market: paper packets on strings, a brass scale, a jar
of bulbs "like onions in coats." *Playable beat:* choose three packets by
tapping; Margo trades each for a button from Wee's pocket, and says something
kind about every choice ("Sweet peas! They climb like they're curious.").
*Garden change:* the bed is dug; garlic and tulip bulbs tucked in under
straw; a small slate label for each packet.

**Ch. 2 — "This little piggie stayed home… and waited for spring."**
Midwinter, frost on the gate, snow on the bed. Hazel's cottage kitchen,
windowsill lined with eggshell pots, El Phantea's teapot steaming. *Playable
beat:* drag a seed from each packet into an eggshell, tap to give it a
blanket of soil; El Phantea waters each one from her teaspoon "with the
solemn care of a duchess." *Garden change:* the bed sleeps under snow while
inside, small green loops push up on the sill — "Look, they're bowing hello."

**Ch. 3 — "This little piggie had roast meef… and radishes on the side."**
Spring, mud, rain, lambs on the far hill. Rocco's outdoor kitchen by the
garden wall, big pot, chalkboard menu. *Playable beat:* tap the leafy tops
in the radish row; each pops up with a soft *plup*, and every row gives
exactly enough for the soup; grown Pipkin carries them to Rocco in a trug.
*Garden change:* sill seedlings planted out, tulips open, garlic standing
tall in green spears.

**Ch. 4 — "This little piggie had none… so everyone shared."**
High summer, bees, long evenings, warm stones. The garden itself, where one
corner square is still bare — the seeds there were tucked in late, "and
that's all right." *Playable beat:* each piggie offers a spare plant (a
sunflower from Hazel, a bean from Rocco, marigolds from Margo); the child
drags each into the empty square and it settles with a little wiggle.
Nothing in the garden is ever "lost" — it's just late, and the friends fill
in. *Garden change:* the whole bed full; sweet peas climbing the gate; a
sunflower looking over the wall.

**Ch. 5 — "And this little piggie cried 'wee wee wee'… all the way home with
seeds for next year."**
Late summer turning to autumn, seedheads papery, light gone gold. The brook
stepping stones from Book One, crossed with a basket of dried flower heads,
ending at the garden gate. *Playable beat:* hop the stones by tapping; each
stone landed on sprouts a tiny flower in the brook's moss, "like a trail of
thanks" — then the seed-keeping finale. *Garden change:* the opening
painting returns, overflowing and going to seed. The year has turned.

**The new mechanic — Wee's Own Square.** In Ch. 1 the child chooses one seed
of their own (sunflower, sweet pea, or pumpkin) and plants it in a marked
square by the gate. That plant **grows between chapters**: each chapter opens
with a short "while you were away" moment — seed (Ch. 1), sprout under snow
(Ch. 2), leaves (Ch. 3), bud (Ch. 4), bloom gone to seed (Ch. 5). Play: *tap*
the plant to water it (three droplets, a soft glassy chime, a happy bounce;
grown Pipkin tips the big watering can alongside); *drag up the stem* to
measure it, a ribbon unrolling while the narrator compares it to Wee ("Up to
Wee's knee!" → "Taller than Wee's ears!"); in Ch. 5, *drag seeds* from the
dry seedhead into a tin with Wee's name painted on it, each landing with a
*tink* — and the tin appears on the final printable keepsake as a
colour-in seed-packet label. The lane's design rule, verbatim in spirit:
**the plant only ever moves forward** — no thirst meter, no wilting art, no
timer; growth is tied to turning the page, not to care, so watering is
affection rather than upkeep. Any tap counts; skipping it entirely still
produces a full bloom, with the narrator saying, *"The rain looked after it
while you were busy."* All five stages are pre-drawn SVG frames — an easy
single-file fit.

**The lane's open questions:** (1) Should the garden persist between visits?
A saved seed tin could open a second playthrough with "last year's seeds" —
but a cleared save must never feel like loss; persistence as a quiet bonus,
never mentioned when absent. (2) Grown Pipkin should be a partner who does
the *second half* of every action (holds the other handle, carries the trug)
— never the whole action, or the child just watches. (3) Chapter 4's piggie
needs Book One's sharing piggie named (the lane left the gap honestly), and:
should Wee narrate the sequel in first person, with the four baked voices
becoming the other piggies' lines and Wee a fifth voice?

> **Sae's pick:** Wee's Own Square, and specifically its rule — *growth tied
> to turning the page, watering as affection rather than upkeep*. It hands
> the sequel an emotional engine the first book didn't have (time passing
> that a child can see and touch), it is failure-free by construction rather
> than by forgiveness, and the seed-tin keepsake closes the year in the
> reader's own hands.

## Strand 5 — Play styles: Origami & the Minimal View *(Gemini lane)*

*Lane note: the first Gemini dispatch failed with a provider error; a single
retry succeeded. The design below is the retry's, complete.*

### Origami as play

**Fold interactions** (gesture → paper → beat):

- **Top-corner snap (ear fold).** Drag the top-right corner to the centre
  guide dot; the sheet folds diagonally on a 45° hinge
  (`rotate3d(1,1,0,…)`, snapping within 40px), crisp paper-rustle sound, a
  10ms haptic pulse. Beat: *"went to market"* — the piggie's first
  travelling ear.
- **Bottom edge up-fold (blanket / snout base).** Drag the bottom edge up to
  the middle guide line; the lower third flips over, revealing the
  peach-coloured underside. Beat: *"stayed home"* — tucks the piggie into
  bed.
- **Accordion pinch (snout).** Two-finger pinch on the centre line; the
  crease puckers outward into a 3D box fold. Beat: *"had roast meef"* — the
  sniffing snout.
- **Spiral twist drag (curly tail).** Drag a small tab in a clockwise arc; a
  narrow strip curls into a spiral tail. Beat: *"had none"* — the tail curls
  (the lane says "in disappointment"; in our book's gentler register this
  reads better as the sharing chapter's thoughtful curl).

**Crease-progress.** 0%: a flat, un-creased square of heavy watercolor paper,
guide lines as faint dotted graphite. 50%: a distinct triangular head
profile, with permanent soft-grey crease lines tracking completed steps.
100%: a fully dimensional, self-standing geometric paper piggie sitting on
the page. **The unfolding payoff:** when the final verse ends, tapping the
completed piggie plays a gentle unfolding — the sheet lies flat once more,
but every crease remains, rendered as glowing golden watercolor ink lines
forming an illustrated map of Piggie's journey home.

**Failure-free rules.** *Wild drag:* the paper skews elastically (up to 30px
off-axis) and rubber-bands back on release, progress untouched. *Wrong
direction:* the crease opens partially and springs back to its folded state
— a fold can be peeked under, never broken. *Impatient double-tap:*
auto-completes the current fold in a smooth 300ms animation with full sound
and haptics, keeping energetic readers moving.

**Palette & material.** Cold-press 300gsm cotton rag with a subtle fibre
texture (CSS `multiply` overlay); backside a soft peach-blush mulberry
(`#F8D7C4`); one accent — warm piglet pink (`#EE7B89`) for guide dots and
active creases; amber-tinted soft shadows that deepen as fold layers stack.

### The Minimal View (bedtime view) — spec

- **Stays:** one full-bleed watercolor painting; the story text block in its
  quiet space; a single faded crescent-moon icon (the exit).
- **Goes:** page-turn arrows, chapter titles, fold guides, game overlays,
  inventory badges, settings menus, progress bars — all of it.
- **Type:** warm serif (Georgia/Palatino class), 2.2rem tablet / 1.6rem
  mobile, line-height 1.7, measure max 32ch, centred.
- **Narration:** read-aloud plays softly on page turn; highlighting is a
  soft-amber text-shadow bleed flowing word by word — no flashing, no
  bounding boxes.
- **Enter/exit:** enter by the crescent moon in the main menu or a two-finger
  tap anywhere; exit by the low-contrast moon (top-left) or a two-finger
  swipe down; 800ms quiet cross-fades both ways.
- **The protection rule — the Zero-Prompt Rule:** no buttons, tooltips,
  hints, animations or interactive elements may ever pop up in Minimal View;
  any touch outside the text simply cross-fades slowly (1000ms) to the next
  page.

> **Sae's pick:** the unfolding payoff — a finished paper piggie that opens
> flat again into a golden crease-map of the journey. It turns progress into
> a keepsake image (the most Storeybrook move in the whole round: the making
> *is* the souvenir), and it gives the Origami look a reason to exist beyond
> dress. Honourable mention: the Zero-Prompt Rule, which should be adopted
> verbatim as the Minimal View's constitution.

## Strand 3 — El Phantea's Tea Letters *(Grok lane)*

**1. Margo (the market).** A market-stall note inked on striped awning
cloth: *"Brought you the last pinch of dusk-rose from the far cart — mind
you don't spill it on the good tablecloth, or the pattern will start telling
stories back."* The card: three stacked market baskets under a striped
awning, in saffron, plum and soft teal, with a tiny brass staple holding a
real folded paper twist of the rose. *Unlock:* a new tea blend at El
Phantea's party — dusk-rose and oat-straw, already measured into the
porcelain trunk.

**2. Wee (the hoofprint letter).** Six neat trot marks and one smudged extra
for excitement: *"I hopped the brook stones twice today and only got one
sock wet; the stones said tell the elephant hello from their wet side."*
The card is pale river-blue with white stone shapes, each stone a raised dot
that can be felt with a fingertip. *Unlock:* a short skipping rhyme the
narration hums later — *"One stone, two stone, three stone, four — mind the
sock and mind the door."*

**3. Rocco (the feast).** Thick cream card, a single roast-meef bone printed
in copper ink: *"Saved the quiet end of the bone for you; it makes a decent
spoon if you hold it right and don't mind the gravy."* A faint steam line
curls into the shape of a tiny cup. *Unlock:* a margin note on the feast
page — *"bone spoons keep tea warm two minutes longer — useful for pocket
elephants."*

**4. Hazel (the cottage).** Cottage linen paper edged with pressed thyme:
*"The kettle's been singing your name since Tuesday; come sit when the wind
turns the same way the curtains do."* The image: a half-open door, lamplight
spilling onto moss-green steps, one curtain stitched with actual thread.
*Unlock:* a seed packet in the story — three thyme seeds tucked behind the
card, for Hazel's window box. (A quiet bridge into the sequel's garden.)

**5. Clove the goat (a new voice, from the market lane).** Tiny bells drawn
in silver on lavender: *"Bells ring softer when the tea is ready; I counted
three rings before the steam stood up straight."* One bell has a real thread
loop. *Unlock:* a gentle three-note hum the narration uses whenever El
Phantea pours.

**6. The brook stones themselves.** Written by water on pale grey paper that
dries crinkled: *"We keep the middle one warm for small feet; tell Pipkin
the green moss is his step now."* A single pressed leaf glued at the corner.
*Unlock:* the hop-home page's stepping-stone illustration changes — the moss
on Pipkin's stone a brighter green.

**The lane's recommendation for first letter:** Margo's — its bright baskets
and real twist of tea introduce the ic.lov postcard style at once, give an
immediate sensory detail, and quietly open the tea-party table before later
letters add songs or seeds.

> **Sae's pick:** the brook stones' letter. A letter *from the brook* is the
> boldest small idea in the bundle — the world itself writing back — and its
> unlock is perfectly scaled: nothing is awarded, the moss is simply greener
> on the stone that is now Pipkin's, forever, every reread. Take Grok's
> ordering advice too (Margo first, for the senses) and let the stones close
> the bundle, so the letters end with the world answering.
