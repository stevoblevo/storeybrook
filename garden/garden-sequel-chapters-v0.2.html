Page oneHome — the autumn the garden began

Fall first, then the year turns. Down by the gate of Wee's cottage is one square of dug earth, waiting — and this is the story of what grew in it.

🌱 Begin the season

Fifteen pages, one garden, no way to spoil any of it. Pipkin — the little round creature, all grown now — will walk beside you, the way he did in the first book.

Page sixWee Weeds — weed, or keep?

Spring, and everything is growing at once — including the things nobody planted. Wee kneels in the square with her trug, and Pipkin, who knows every leaf by sight, advises.

Sort each newcomer: into the square if it is a friend, or down by the fence if it is a weed. Weeds are not villains — the fence corner gets rain too. There is no way to sort wrongly for long; Pipkin will always tell you true.

Page twelveBirds — share, or shoo kindly

A flower full of seeds is a rumour, and birds always hear rumours first. Three of them land on the fence, polite as anything, and wait to see what kind of garden this is.

Both choices are kind ones, and the garden has enough for either. Share a few seeds now — or wave Pipkin's ribbon, gently, and let the birds sing from the tree instead. Choose as you like; you cannot choose wrong.

Page fifteenHome by the Brook — the season's rhyme

And this little piggie cried "wee, wee, wee!" all the way home — by the brook, as the light failed, with a tin of seeds for next year knocking softly in her pocket.

The year has turned: the opening painting, a year later, the bed full and gone to seed. Tap the fireflies, keep the rhyme, and come back whenever — the garden remembers its friends.

The season's rhyme — a keepsake
This little piggie went to market, for seeds in paper packets bright; this little piggie stayed home, and kept them warm all night; this little piggie planted hers, in her own square by the gate; this little piggie had none — so the others shared, and filled her plate; and this little piggie cried "wee, wee, wee!" all the way home by the brook, with seeds for next year.
