The Sixth Piggie · A clue at the brook

Five stones home. One stone, apart.

The way home crosses the brook on five stepping stones, the way it always has. But beneath the willow, a little apart from the route, one more stone sits in the shade — dry, quiet, and kept for small feet.

🌿 beneath the willow, where the water runs slow 🌿
five stones home — and one stone, apart

The brook, on the way home

On the dry stone: a blue ribbon, laid carefully, and two small hoofmarks close together — as if someone stood here a little while, deciding whether to come closer.

〜 〜 〜 🏡✨

The water shimmers. Nothing else, yet.

The same blue as the feast napkin, the thread on the well's lowest stone, the thread in Hazel's unfinished pear, and the ribbon on the tea party's little chair. Five clues, one colour, one small guest — and this stone is her door.

Walk the five stones home if you like — they are all still there, and the way home is unchanged. Or touch the stone apart, beneath the willow, and see what its reflection has been keeping.

🏡 Beneath the willow at dusk, the reflection opens into a low, round room of roots, and through its leafy ceiling you can still see the brook's stones. A kettle stands on a flat pebble; six mismatched cups wait on a cloth. A small, soft-grey piggie with one ear folded forward looks up and says: “You can sit down while I think.” The door was never locked — only uncounted. And on the keepsake's reverse, a seventh cup waits, for the reader, whenever they have a little while.
